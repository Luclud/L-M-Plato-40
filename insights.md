### PLATO-P1 — Floor above the car park runs cold

- **Hypothesis:** P1 Floor 1 (above the open car park) runs colder in winter → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: occupied floor-1 rooms are below 20 °C in 17.3 % of winter intervals vs 9.8 %–11.3 % on floors 2, 3, 4 (n=177,083 occupied, window-closed intervals). Controlled for orientation x room size the floor-1 excess is +6.8 pp (worse in 5/6 strata: N/22 m²: 10.2 % vs 16.1 %; N/28 m²: 20.8 % vs 9.6 %; N/32 m²: 12.3 % vs 7.9 %; S/22 m²: 16.3 % vs 9.7 %; S/28 m²: 23.8 % vs 9.8 %; S/32 m²: 18.3 % vs 7.3 %). Setpoints do not explain it (mean 21.31 °C floor 1 vs 21.20 °C others; mean temperature 20.76 vs 21.13 °C). By daily mean outdoor temperature (heating season): -40..-5 °C: 26.3 % vs 11.3 %; -5..0 °C: 17.4 % vs 11.0 %; 0..5 °C: 17.1 % vs 9.6 %; 5..10 °C: 14.1 % vs 9.0 %; 10..40 °C: 15.6 % vs 5.6 %.
- **Evidence:** `room_telemetry.csv: temperature_c, window_open=0, local months 12,1,2, 2026-01-01..2027-01-01`; `events.jsonl: stays (checkin*/stay_in_progress -> checkout*) via plato40.state.State = occupied`; `building.json: floor, orientation, area_m2 per room`
- **Method:** Share of occupied 15-min intervals with temperature_c < 20 °C, occupancy from stays (not setpoint proxy), open-window intervals excluded, stratified by orientation x area_m2, cross-tab by daily mean outdoor temperature.
- **Recommendation:** Next PLATO: insulate the floor slab over the open car park (soffit insulation / heated soffit) and give floor-1 modules a larger emitter or own heating zone; CLARA meanwhile: +0.5 °C setpoint offset for floor-1 occupied rooms in the heating season (stays within the 20–23 °C band).
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: fewer cold intervals on floor 1 (would close most of a 6.8 pp gap); no savings claimed.
- **Uncertainty:** Synthetic, uncalibrated thermal model; floor effect is confounded with any other floor-1 specific factor (e.g. entrance/lobby proximity) that the data does not describe; motion-free sleeping guests still count as occupied (correct for comfort). Confidence 0.75.

### PLATO-P2 — Summer overheating without cooling

- **Hypothesis:** P2 Summer overheating in a heating-only building (south worse) → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40 (heating only, no cooling asset): occupied rooms exceed 26 °C in 32.5 % of summer intervals and are above the 23 °C comfort band in 67.7 % (n=237,545); 1,368 occupied room-days with > 26 °C. South 36.1 % vs north 29.0 %; by floor 1: 28.6 %, 2: 33.3 %, 3: 36.3 %, 4: 32.0 %. Vacant rooms also reach > 26 °C in 23.0 % -> the envelope/solar gain, not guests, drives it. Guests sent 201 temperature_hot vs 29 temperature_cold requests (hot by orientation {'S': 117, 'N': 84}, rooms per orientation {'S': 20, 'N': 20}); BMS raised 125 ROOM_TEMP_HIGH alarms.
- **Evidence:** `room_telemetry.csv: temperature_c, local months 6,7,8, 2026-01-01..2027-01-01`; `events.jsonl: stays via State = occupied; bms_alarm ROOM_TEMP_HIGH raised`; `guest_requests.csv: request_type temperature_hot / temperature_cold by room`; `building.json: orientation; assets (no cooling asset)`
- **Method:** Share of occupied summer intervals above 26 °C and above 23 °C; split by orientation and floor; vacant rooms as control; guest temperature requests by orientation.
- **Recommendation:** Next PLATO: external shading (fixed overhangs / exterior blinds) on south facades as standard, plus a passive/low-energy cooling option in the module catalogue (e.g. ceiling fans, reversible heat pump with night free cooling). CLARA meanwhile: forecast-driven night flushing and closing blinds on south rooms before hot afternoons.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: fewer hot complaints and alarm-free summer comfort; no savings or temperature reductions claimed (replayed telemetry cannot react).
- **Uncertainty:** Synthetic solar/thermal model; 26 °C is our comfort threshold (the BMS alarms at ~28 °C); guest request counts are small per room. Confidence 0.80.

### PLATO-P3 — Rooftop plant needs permanent access

- **Hypothesis:** P3 Rooftop AHU needs a lift platform -> postponed filter changes → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: 2 AHU1 filter changes were postponed, 2 explicitly because the rooftop unit is only reachable with a lift platform; the filter change happened 16, 12 days later (dp then 202, 234 Pa). 2 further unplanned 'heavily loaded filter' replacements by a contractor followed 10 FILTER_DP_HIGH and 9 AIRFLOW_LOW alarms (alarm dp ≥ 260 Pa). Every postponement is filter-life consumed at reduced airflow and higher fan power.
- **Evidence:** `work_orders.csv: asset_id=AHU1, status=postponed / type=corrective (summary text)`; `equipment.csv: AHU1 filter_dp_pa, airflow_m3h (daily median at usual airflow)`; `events.jsonl: bms_alarm FILTER_DP_HIGH / AIRFLOW_LOW raised`
- **Method:** Text match on AHU1 work orders; filter changes detected as > 30 % day-over-day drops of the daily median filter_dp_pa at the usual airflow; delay = postponed work order -> detected change.
- **Recommendation:** Next PLATO: design for maintainability — fixed roof access (stair/ladder + guarded walkway) or place the AHU at ground/technical-room level so filters can be swapped by the in-house technician without a lift platform; filter-door side reachable from the walkway.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: no access-related postponements (here 2), fewer contractor call-outs (here 2 unplanned); no cost figures claimed.
- **Uncertainty:** Work-order texts are terse and written by people; two postponements is a small sample; synthetic AHU model. Confidence 0.70.

### PLATO-P4 — Sub-meter risers and the technical room

- **Hypothesis:** P4 Water sub-metering per riser / technical room → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: the unmetered gap main − Σ rooms is 329 L/day (median, IQR 298–350). 1 multi-day gap excursions: 2026-07-11..2026-07-17 (7 d, +593 L/day over a 499 L/day baseline, ≈4153 L total, room meters +2 % vs before, work orders ['WO00040'], WATER_NIGHT_FLOW 0). 1/1 match a WM_MAIN repair — a leak in common pipework that no room meter sees and the BMS night-flow rule did not flag. Room-side leaks (4 work orders) are visible on room meters, common-area leaks only as a few hundred L/day on a meter that also carries irrigation.
- **Evidence:** `water_main.csv + room_water.csv: daily use (valid readings, resets = NaN) -> gap = main − Σ rooms`; `work_orders.csv: corrective work orders on WM_MAIN / rooms (leak texts)`; `events.jsonl: bms_alarm WATER_NIGHT_FLOW raised`
- **Method:** Daily gap vs trailing 28-day median (shifted, history only); excursion = ≥ 3 days with excess > max(250 L, 4×MAD); matched to WM_MAIN work orders created within the excursion + 3 days.
- **Recommendation:** Next PLATO: sub-meter each riser, the technical room (DHW circulation / plant) and irrigation separately, so every litre of the main meter is attributed to a zone; add a leak sensor in the technical room. CLARA meanwhile: daily gap-baseline rule (this method) as a water-guard trigger.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: common-area leaks localised in hours instead of days; no water/cost savings claimed.
- **Uncertainty:** Gap baseline drifts with season (irrigation, common use); meter jitter/resets add noise; one confirmed gap leak is a small sample. Confidence 0.75.

### PLATO-P5 — Redundant sensor gateways per floor

- **Hypothesis:** P5 Gateway reliability per floor -> placement / redundancy → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: gateway outages (all rooms of a gateway silent) — GW_F1: 3 outages, 9.0 h, room-level missing 0.32 %; GW_F2: 0 outages, 0.0 h, room-level missing 0.48 %; GW_F3: 11 outages, 52.8 h, room-level missing 0.91 %; GW_F4: 0 outages, 0.0 h, room-level missing 0.37 %. GW_F3 accounts for 85.4 % of outage hours and 114/159 SENSOR_COMM_LOSS alarms. A floor gateway is a single point of failure: during an outage every room on that floor is unknown (no comfort, presence or leak evidence).
- **Evidence:** `room_telemetry.csv: intervals where all rooms of a gateway lack temperature_c (gateway outage)`; `building.json: sensors[].gateway (room -> gateway)`; `events.jsonl: bms_alarm SENSOR_COMM_LOSS raised by room`
- **Method:** Outage = 15-min interval where every room mapped to the gateway has temperature_c missing; SENSOR_COMM_LOSS raised alarms mapped room -> gateway.
- **Recommendation:** Next PLATO: two gateways per floor with overlapping coverage (or a meshed sensor network), gateway on UPS, and check the radio path/placement of GW_F3 before copying the floor layout; CLARA: treat a gateway outage as 'unknown' for every room on the floor and raise one data-quality episode, not per-room alarms.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: no floor-wide blind spots; fewer per-room comm-loss alarms; no cost figures claimed.
- **Uncertainty:** Cause of outages (placement, power, interference) is not in the data; synthetic outage model; few events. Confidence 0.65.

### CLARA-C1 — Rotate room allocation to avoid stagnant water

- **Hypothesis:** C1 Room allocation rotation to avoid long vacancy (legionella flushes) → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: 11 legionella flushing work orders covered 38 room flushes in 24 rooms (most often R110 4x, R105 3x, R306 3x, R108 3x). Occupancy from stays shows 81 vacancy runs ≥ 7 days in 36 rooms (longest 15.4 days) although room occupancy shares only range 56%–65%; in 81/81 of these runs another room of the same size received a check-in before day 7, i.e. rotation could have used the idle room.
- **Evidence:** `work_orders.csv: 'Legionella prevention: taps flushed in long-vacant rooms ...' (room ids in text)`; `events.jsonl: stays via State (vacancy runs per room)`; `building.json: area_m2 (room type)`
- **Method:** Vacancy runs = consecutive unoccupied 15-min intervals per room (stays from events); rotation potential = a same-area_m2 check-in elsewhere within the first 7 days of the run (simulation, not replayed).
- **Recommendation:** CLARA rule: when allocating a booking, prefer the clean, released room of the requested type with the longest current vacancy; if a room still reaches 5 vacant days, schedule an autonomous tap flush during the next housekeeping visit instead of a separate contractor visit.
- **Expected benefit:** Simulated estimate: up to 81 of 81 long vacancies avoidable by rotation (labelled simulation on historic allocations; no change to replayed data).
- **Uncertainty:** Allocation constraints (guest preferences, accessibility, room blocks) are not in the data; flush policy threshold of the operator is unknown (work orders list rooms only). Confidence 0.60.

### CLARA-C2 — Retune BMS comfort and water alarms

- **Hypothesis:** C2 BMS alarm tuning: volume vs real incidents → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: the BMS raised 464 alarms ({'ROOM_TEMP_LOW': 160, 'SENSOR_COMM_LOSS': 159, 'ROOM_TEMP_HIGH': 125, 'FILTER_DP_HIGH': 10, 'AIRFLOW_LOW': 9, 'WATER_NIGHT_FLOW': 1}); only 2.5 % of ROOM_TEMP_LOW alarms (fires at ≤ 17.5 °C) concern an occupied room — the rest are setback rooms; meanwhile occupied rooms were below 20 °C on 1,937 room-days, of which only 41 had a ROOM_TEMP_LOW alarm. WATER_NIGHT_FLOW fired 1x and preceded 1/5 leak repairs within 14 days.
- **Evidence:** `events.jsonl: bms_alarm (alarm_code, state, asset_id, value)`; `room_telemetry.csv + stays: occupied room-days below 20 °C`; `work_orders.csv: leak / burst / fill-valve / refrigerant work orders`
- **Method:** ROOM_TEMP_LOW raised events joined to stays at the alarm time; occupied cold room-days from telemetry; WATER_NIGHT_FLOW raised within 14 days before leak work orders (text match).
- **Recommendation:** CLARA: make comfort alarms occupancy-aware — suppress ROOM_TEMP_LOW for vacant setback rooms (unless < 12 °C frost risk) and alarm occupied rooms at < 20 °C for ≥ 1 h (and arriving rooms 2 h before check-in); replace the single main-meter night-flow rule by per-room continuous-flow and main-minus-rooms gap rules (see PLATO-P4) evaluated by the water agent; keep BMS alarms as evidence, not as triggers.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: far fewer irrelevant comfort alarms, earlier detection of real occupied-room cold and leaks; alarm volume after retuning not simulated.
- **Uncertainty:** BMS alarm logic is unknown (delayed/incomplete by design); leak work-order matching by text; 'cold' uses our 20 °C band threshold. Confidence 0.80.

### CLARA-C3 — Pre-heating before forecast cold snaps

- **Hypothesis:** C3 Pre-heating curve before forecast cold snaps → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: the occupied share below 20 °C rises as days get colder (-40..-5 °C: 15.0 %; -5..0 °C: 12.6 %; 0..5 °C: 11.4 %; 5..10 °C: 10.3 %; 10..40 °C: 8.0 %), while HP1 COP falls (-40..-5 °C: 2.27; -5..0 °C: 2.65; 0..5 °C: 3.01; 5..10 °C: 3.39; 10..40 °C: 3.99). The 06:00-local forecast is good enough to act on: MAE 0–12 h: 0.82 K, 12–24 h: 1.45 K, 24–48 h: 2.33 K. HP1 logged 17 lockout intervals (median outdoor -1.1 °C); 2/2 corrective HP1 work orders were preceded by lockouts 2.0, 15.4 days earlier.
- **Evidence:** `weather.csv / weather_forecast.csv: outdoor_c vs outdoor_c_forecast by lead time, 2026-01-01..2027-01-01`; `equipment.csv: HP1 heat_output_kw / electric_kw (COP), status lockout_*`; `room_telemetry.csv + stays: occupied share < 20 °C by daily mean outdoor temperature`; `work_orders.csv: HP1 corrective (refrigerant) work orders`
- **Method:** Occupied cold share binned by local daily mean outdoor temperature; COP = heat_output_kw / electric_kw while running; forecast MAE vs measured hourly mean by lead time; lockouts in the 21 days before HP1 corrective work orders.
- **Recommendation:** CLARA rule: when the latest forecast shows a daily mean outdoor temperature ≤ -5 °C within 12–24 h, pre-heat occupied and arriving rooms to the upper comfort band (22–23 °C) during the cheapest hours before the drop, run DHW charging ahead of the cold period, and keep vacant rooms at setback. Treat a first HP1 lockout_* status in a cold spell as an FDD trigger for a technician request (approval) instead of waiting for guest complaints.
- **Expected benefit:** Simulated/qualitative: fewer occupied cold intervals during cold snaps and earlier refrigerant-leak detection; energy cost impact not quantified (pre-heating can shift load to cheap hours but may add kWh).
- **Uncertainty:** Correlational (cold days also coincide with HP faults in this dataset); pre-heat effect cannot be measured on replayed telemetry; synthetic heat-pump and forecast-error models. Confidence 0.60.

### CLARA-C4 — Condition-based filter interval

- **Hypothesis:** C4 Filter maintenance interval from dp trend instead of fixed dates → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: 5 AHU1 filter cycles detected; clean filter ≈ 90 Pa, loading rate 1.48–2.76 Pa/day (median 1.51). At these rates the BMS FILTER_DP_HIGH level (≈ 260 Pa) is reached after 62–115 days (median 105); observed cycles lasted 69, 97, 36, 81 days. Fixed planned work orders came at intervals of 9, 91, 5 days, and the fastest-loading cycles still hit the alarm before the next planned change (10 FILTER_DP_HIGH alarms).
- **Evidence:** `equipment.csv: AHU1 filter_dp_pa at usual airflow (daily median), airflow_m3h`; `events.jsonl: bms_alarm FILTER_DP_HIGH raised (value = alarm dp)`; `work_orders.csv: AHU1 preventive / corrective filter work orders`
- **Method:** Daily median filter_dp_pa restricted to the usual airflow (±3 %), cycles split at > 30 % drops, linear loading rate per cycle, days to threshold = (alarm dp − clean dp) / rate.
- **Recommendation:** CLARA rule: forecast days-to-threshold from the causal rolling dp slope (last 14 days at usual airflow) and create the filter work order when the projection is < 21 days — book the technician (and, until P3 is fixed, the lift platform) 14 days ahead; distinguish a dp gap (missing data) from loading.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: changes happen before FILTER_DP_HIGH / AIRFLOW_LOW instead of after; fewer unplanned contractor replacements.
- **Uncertainty:** Synthetic, near-linear loading model; boost-airflow periods excluded (dp rises with flow); the alarm level is taken from the BMS alarm values, not a manufacturer limit. Confidence 0.70.

### CLARA-C5 — Learned cleaning durations, robot pre-clean, sick-day buffer

- **Hypothesis:** C5 Cleaning strategy: learned durations, robot pre-clean, sick-day buffer → **confirmed**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: human room cleaning takes median/P90 22 m² 45/45 min (n=1481), 28 m² 45/60 min (n=1501), 32 m² 45/60 min (n=732); when a robot floor-cleaned the room in the 4 h before, the median is (22 m²: 45 → 45 min; 28 m²: 60 → 45 min; 32 m²: 60 → 45 min). 24 guests waited for their room (median 45 min, max 750 min); 15 of them on the 24 days with a cleaner absent (0.62 waits/day vs 0.03 on other days). Absences became known only 2.1 h (median) before shift start. Days with ≥ 16 check-ins (top 10 %) have 0.40 waits/day vs 0.02.
- **Evidence:** `events.jsonl: cleaning_started -> cleaning_completed per room (duration), robot_floor_cleaning_started`; `events.jsonl: arrival_waiting_room -> checkin_after_wait (wait), checkin* (arrivals per local day)`; `staff_roster.csv: cleaner shifts with status != scheduled and status_known_utc`
- **Method:** cleaning_started matched to the next cleaning_completed of the room (≤ 6 h); robot pre-clean = robot_floor_cleaning_started for that room ≤ 4 h before; waits matched to checkin_after_wait by booking.
- **Recommendation:** CLARA: plan turnarounds with the learned P90 duration per room size (not a flat slot); dispatch robots to pre-clean vacated larger rooms first; accept early check-in only when a cleaner slot finishes ≥ 30 min before the promised time; on days in the top-10 % arrival band pre-book ONCALL_1 and resequence cleaning as soon as a sickness notice arrives.
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative / simulated: fewer waiting guests on sick and peak days; robot effect is observational, not causal.
- **Uncertainty:** Robot pre-clean is not randomised (scheduling may favour easier rooms); durations are quantised to 15 min; only 24 waits in the period -> rates are noisy. Confidence 0.65.

### CLARA-C6 — Occupancy-driven restock and extra pickups

- **Hypothesis:** C6 Waste / vending service frequency vs occupancy (robots carrying bins) → **qualified**
- **Finding:** PLATO-40: VEND1 ran out of stock 20x ({'drinks': 20}), 14 of them on Sundays, on days with mean occupancy 69.5 % vs 60.0 % overall. WASTE_MAIN sat at 100 % for 72 hours (3 waste_bin_full events). Floor bins: max 63 %, P95 36 % — the hypothesis that robots should carry floor bins is rejected for PLATO-40; the bottleneck is the central container and the vending restock rhythm.
- **Evidence:** `logistics.csv: WASTE_F*/WASTE_MAIN fill_pct, VEND1 stock (hourly)`; `events.jsonl: vending_out_of_stock, waste_bin_full, floor_bin_emptied, waste_pickup, vending_restocked`; `room_telemetry.csv + stays: occupied share per day`
- **Method:** Counts of logistics events by local weekday; hourly fill/stock distributions; occupancy from stays.
- **Recommendation:** CLARA: order an extra vending restock (24 h lead) when forecast occupancy for the days until the next regular restock exceeds the yearly median and stock is projected to run out; order an extra WASTE_MAIN pickup when the fill trend projects 100 % before the next regular pickup; keep floor-bin emptying with housekeeping (no robot bin runs needed).
- **Expected benefit:** Qualitative: fewer empty-vending and overflowing-container hours; extra service costs need approval if notable.
- **Uncertainty:** Synthetic consumption models; fill values capped at 100 % (overflow volume unknown); few events. Confidence 0.60.

## Limitations

- All findings concern the fictional **PLATO-40** and one synthetic, uncalibrated dataset — not real PLATO
  buildings or CLARA installations. Effects are correlational; nothing here was tested by intervention.
- This is a retrospective analysis of the whole period (allowed for learning, unlike the decision pipeline);
  the CLARA rules it proposes are written so they can run causally (trailing baselines, forecasts as issued).
- Expected benefits are qualitative or labelled *simulated*; no savings are claimed.
- Thresholds (20 °C, 26 °C, 7-day vacancy, gap baseline) are our choices; numbers are regenerated from the data
  each run, so the holdout or next year's data can confirm or overturn them.
- BMS alarms and work orders are human/rule outputs (late, terse, incomplete) and only weak evidence.
